## Experimental design

About a month before the assembly of the experiment, the basal resources and preys are raised.

## Chaoborus

**Initial conditions.** Chaoborus will be sourced in the form of third-instar larvae *(add the size of the larvae and references that say that's a third instar)* from IT-Ichthyo Trophic, Poland. The initial population density of Chaoborus will be 10, which is above what needed for all the different analyses (4.5 mg, see [Box 1: Sample Collection and Storage](protocols/sample_collection_and_storage.md)). This larval density, which below 50 individuals per jar (9 cm diameter) corresponds to ~8,000 individuals m⁻², which falls within the upper range of peak natural Chaoborus densities reported from eutrophic, fish-free systems (Xie et al., 1998). Chaoborus will be fed ad libitum by providing them with 10 Daphnia of 1 to 5 days old (see [Box 1: Sample Collection and Storage](protocols/sample_collection_and_storage.md)).

**Measurements.** At the beginning of the experiment and through the experiment we will be measuring end points telling us about the quantity, quality, and timing of resource flows (see Table 2). We will pull larvae, pupae, and adults together, recording the number of larvae, pupae, and adults. During this, we will also be taking out all the extra Daphnia in the jars *(add whether should we do this every day or every time we change the medium of Chaoborus)*. **Stock populations.** At the beginning of the experiment, Daphnia magna we will increase the amount of stock cultures *(add where do cultures come from)* kept in 0.8 mL of COMBO Medium (without nutrients — phosphorus and nitrogen — to stop the growth of algae) in 1 litre jars. We are aiming at having about 15 adult Daphnia per jar *(add why this number and how we keep this number)*. Every two days we change the medium.

**Creation of single-clutch jars.** Daphnia will be monitored for when they give birth. Throughout the experiment, we will check every *(add how often)* if an adult has given babies. If it has, we will take it out and write when the clutches were given so that we can make sure we are giving two-day-old babies *(add whether you think if maybe it would be better to have another age of the babies, say if it reduces the amount of work)*. If the adult is pregnant, it will probably hatch within the next 24 hours. On the day of the assembly, pregnant Daphnia are transferred in *(add number of jars per food item)* 100 mL jars with food, which was one of the three algal diet treatments: Microcystis aeruginosa, Synechococcus elongatus, Nannochloropsis limnetica. Starting from the day of the experiment, every day we feed Daphnia. This is realistic in nature compared to the percentage of biomass made out of cyanobacteria (ref) (bloom defined as > 50% of biomass constituted by cyanobacteria, Molot et al., 2014).
