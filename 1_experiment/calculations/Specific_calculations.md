> ### Calculating biomass
>
> Calculating how much biomass is needed requires calculating how much biodilution occurs along the food chain from the literature:
>
> **(i) _Microcystis aeruginosa_: 0.7–7 mg g⁻¹ DW.**
>
> The density of Microcystins inside _M. aeruginosa_ can depend on the growth phase considered. In an experiment where PCC 7806 was raised in modified MLA medium with little nitrogen, the amount of toxins were between 0.7 and 7 mg g⁻¹ DW (Long et al., 2001).
>
> **(ii) _Daphnia magna_: biodilution of 80 times.**
>
> In an experiment where _M. aeruginosa_ produced about 2 mg g⁻¹ DW of cyanotoxins, _D. magna_ had about 0.025 mg g⁻¹ DW (Thostrup & Christoffersen, 1999).
>
> **(iii) _Chaoborus_ sp.: biodilution of 5 times.**
>
> A lab study and two field studies consistently show a biodilution effect of microcystins from cyanobacteria to _Chaoborus_. A lab study showed that Microcystins could not be found in _Chaoborus_ fed with Microcystis-exposed Daphnia (Laurén-Määttä et al., 1995). However, this study has two limitations:
>
> 1. it's possible that there were toxins in _Chaoborus_ which were not detected, as they used HPLC, which has high detection limits; and
> 2. they measured only toxins in _Chaoborus_ but not _Daphnia_, raising question of whether _Daphnia_ itself might not have accumulated toxins.
>
> Two field studies also have opposite results. A field study (with HPLC/PDA) supports accumulation of Microcystins in _Chaoborus_ where there is a biodilution of about five times from zooplankton to _Chaoborus_ (Microcystins biodilute from 863 ng g⁻¹ FW in zooplankton to 168 ng g⁻¹ FW to _Chaoborus_ larvae, which I calculated from the fresh weight of 17.26 µg g⁻¹ DW to 3.36 µg g⁻¹ DW in _Chaoborus_, as both zooplankton and _Chaoborus_ are about 95% water) (Sotton et al., 2014). Another field study (with high resolution LC–MS/MS) shows that zooplankton contained Microcystins (2.5–4.5 µg g⁻¹ DW), but not _Chaoborus_ (Rihelle et al., 2024).
>
