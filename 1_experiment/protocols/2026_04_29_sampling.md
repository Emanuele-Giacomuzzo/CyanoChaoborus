# Sample the Last Time Point

**Date:** Wednesday, 29 April 2026

**Aim:** Sampling the last time point of the experiment.

## Sample the algae

1. Prepare a mix of the two algae.
   1. Bring up the large algae culture.
   2. Take out algae into two Beckers.
   3. Replace the COMBO of the algae with Zoo COMBO. For each alga:
      1. Add 48 ml of algae to <mark>8</mark> centrifuge tubes (<u>use the auto pipette and the pipette tip with written the name of the species of algae you are working with</u>).
      2. Transform the inoculated algae into a pellet by spinning the Falcon tubes for 10 minutes at 28,000 revolutions per minute.
      3. Meanwhile you are waiting for the centrifuge to be done, turn on the spectrophotometer.
      4. Throw out the liquid (phytoplankton COMBO) from the algal cultures. <u>Do this as quickly as possible after the spinning to avoid resuspension.</u>
      5. Add 48 ml of Zoo COMBO to each of the falcon tubes. Then mix them into a Becker.
   4. Measure the optical density (OD) of the algae using a spectrophotometer (needs 15 minutes from when you turn it on to when you can use it). <u>The cuvette is kept inside ethanol and is washed first with demi water and then again with ethanol.</u> The cuvette is cleaned with specific paper.
   5. Measure Zoo-COMBO alone by selecting photometric > enter.
   6. Measure the algae by selecting GOTOWL: 480 nm; start.
   7. Mix the algae so that half of carbon comes from *Tetradesmus* and half comes from *Synechococcus*.
2. Set up the filtration material.
   1. Set up the vacuum filtration pump and connect its tube to the wall gas supply (the tube sucks air in, allowing the liquids to come more easily through the filter).
   2. Add GF/C filters (<u>they should have been burnt at 550 °C for 2 hours</u>) on the filtration unit using <u>clean forceps</u> (cleaned with ethanol and fire — hold forceps horizontally while burning).
   3. Add cylinder weights on top of the filters.
   4. Turn on suction slowly, checking on the filtration unit that the pressure doesn't exceed 0.2 bar.
3. Add samples to the filters. For each of the samples:
   1. Pipette culture on the filter (remember to mix well the sample, and make sure that the liquid goes through, otherwise adjust the tap until no liquid is left on top of the filter). <u>Write down how much volume you added to the filter:</u>

      **Volume added:** ______________
   2. Move the filters to a 24-well plate. To do so, turn off the suction, take off the cylinder weights, and move filters to the 24-well plate with <u>clean forceps</u> (cleaned with ethanol and fire).[^1]
   3. Clean. Put back cylinder weights and turn on suction. Then, clean meshes and cylinders by pouring ethanol and demineralised water on them.
   4. Place 24-well plate with filters inside in an oven at 50 °C overnight to dry.

## Sample the Daphnia

1. Sample Daphnias left from the Chaoborus jars. Filter the Daphnia left in each of the replicates and keep them in a Falcon tube with 70% ethanol.
2. Sample 1000 Daphnia from the cohorts started five days ago. Keep them in their original COMBO.
3. Divide small and large Daphnias using a 1 mm mesh.
4. Store 20 small and 20 large in ethanol to later analyse their body size to see if they are different.
