# Box 1: Measuring, Storing, and Freeze-Drying Samples Throughout the Experiment

Referenced from [experimental_design.md](../experimental_design.md).

Every day we'll be looking at the following things:

1. **Emerged Chaoborus adults**

   Once adults have emerged, we'll take them out and measure their body size. To do so, we'll take them with forceps and put them into Eppendorf tubes which we'll put into the fridge to kill them. Once they are dead, we'll measure their body size *(add whether under the microscope)* and then put them into the freezer. But then it seems like we are going to need a different set of tubes, as otherwise the other ones will be with other individuals.

2. **Dead Chaoborus larvae**

   During the experiment we want to take care of all the samples we need to take both in terms of the adults and the larvae of Chaoborus, but also maybe of Daphnia and the algae, but let's see. Okay so what we want to do is to really go for the samples to be taken into the different Eppendorf tubes which will be in the freezer. So, what we want to do is whenever we have to take samples to be stored, we will put them into some Eppendorf tubes which are labelled with the different things. Every time we put a new individual inside, we should have tally marks on them (卌) to keep track of how many are in each sample. About 50 Chaoborus larvae can be stored in 1.5 mL Eppendorf tubes, with the new tubes being 2 mL, therefore bringing up this number even higher.

3. **Taken all together**

   Take out the water from the samples using a Pasteur pipette.

   Okay, so now let's think about how to split the samples. So, we will have for each sample:

   E.g., samples for Chaoborus in bad quality treatment:
   - CN (to send to Dominik)
   - P (done in-house)
   - Sterols
   - Fatty acids
   - Toxicity

   This should be applied to five organisms, which are: Chaoborus adults, Chaoborus larvae, Daphnia, algae *(add whether we should measure Daphnia and algae and how often)*. Which means we should have 250 Eppendorf tubes:

   **4 Treatments × 4 Replicates × 5 Analyses × 4 Organisms = 320 Eppendorf tubes**

   To do so, we need to basically go about it by taking the different individuals and putting them inside the tubes one after the other, one individual per analysis and then go back to it. For example, individual 1 goes in CN, 2 in P, 3 in sterols, 4 in fatty acids, 5 in toxicity, 6 in CN, 7 in P, etc.
