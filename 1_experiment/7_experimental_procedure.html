Experimental Procedure

Grow species

Before starting the experiment, we need to grow the following species:

  • Phytoplankton
    • Microcystis aeruginosa (enough for toxins)
    • Synechococcus elongatus
    • Nannochloropsis limnetica
    • Tetradesmus obliquus
  • Daphnia magna

Phytoplankton

All downstream feeding of Daphnia (and sampling of phytoplankton) depends on having enough phytoplankton biomass, of every one of the r Phyto_species_count species, including enough M. aeruginosa to add toxins to the cultures. Experimental cultures can’t be started directly at experimental scale, though, so they have to be built up gradually from a small stock.

To do that, each species is scaled up through three vessel sizes in sequence:

  1. Stock flask (max capacity: r Phyto_stock_flask_liquid_mL ml), with r Phyto_stock_flask_liquid_mL ml of algae incoulated from r Phyto_stock_flask_inoculation_mL ml of previous stock flasks
  2. Stock bottle (max capacity: r Phyto_stock_bottle_capacity_mL ml), with r Phyto_stock_bottle_capacity_mL ml of algae incoulated from r Phyto_stock_bottle_inoculation_mL ml of stock flasks
  3. Experimental bottle (max capacity: r Phyto_experimental_bottle_liquid_mL ml), with r Phyto_experimental_bottle_liquid_mL ml of algae incoulated from r Phyto_stock_bottle_inoculation_mL ml of stock bottles.

The experimental bottle is where we will be harvesting the algae to feed Daphnia, so it has to be kept at a high enough density to meet that demand. Every r NA days the algae are harvested and fed to Daphnia / lysed and provided to Chaoborus, while replacing r NA % of the bottles with COMBO medium. Each bottle is kept for r NA days before it’s replaced. Also, each experimental bottle is grown r NA days before to be able to be used as it needs to grow to enough density.

Scaled to r Phyto_species_count species × r Phyto_vessels_per_species replicates (r Phyto_experimental_bottle_nr lines total), that means preparing in advance:

  • r Phyto_experimental_bottle_nr × r Phyto_stock_flask_liquid_mL mL stock flasks
  • r Phyto_experimental_bottle_nr × r Phyto_stock_bottle_capacity_mL mL stock bottles (holding r Phyto_stock_bottle_liquid_mL mL of liquid)
  • r Phyto_experimental_bottle_nr × r round(Phyto_experimental_bottle_liquid_mL / 1000, 1) L experimental bottles
  • r round(COMBO_total_litres, 1) L of COMBO medium, made in r COMBO_batch_volume_litres L batches (r COMBO_batches batches; see the COMBO protocol)

Daphnia

Daphnia is raised with r Daphnia_adults_per_new_cohort_jar adults in 1 L jars with r Large_jar_liquid_litres L Zoo-COMBO medium. Daphnia will be grown for r Daphnia_max_age_eaten_days days to give babies, which will then be fed to Chaoborus. To avoid Daphnia become too old to be fertile enough, Daphnia will be raised from adults from the stock populations every time. To avoid that the largest Daphnias are picked for earlier cohorts, causing a decrease in the offspring count in the later cohorts, we will pick Daphnias of different sizes. Daphnia magna is fed ad libitum with 2 mg C/L/day [@lampert1978].

Each cohort has to be sized to cover Chaoborus’ worst-case daily appetite. Across r Treatments treatments × r Replicates replicates, with r Chaoborus_larvae_per_jar larvae per jar each capable of a single-feeding maximum of r Chaoborus_Daphnia_neonates_eaten_max_recorded neonates, that demand is r Chaoborus_Daphnia_neonates_needed_per_day neonates per day. A single cohort jar, started with r Daphnia_adults_per_new_cohort_jar adults each producing r Daphnia_offsprings_per_mother_per_5_days neonates over the r Daphnia_max_age_eaten_days-day cohort cycle, yields about r Daphnia_neonates_per_cohort_jar neonates by harvest day — so each daily cohort needs r Daphnia_jars_per_cohort jars to keep up with Chaoborus.

Because a new cohort is started every day and takes r Daphnia_max_age_eaten_days days to reach harvest age, r Daphnia_cohorts_in_rotation cohorts of staggered ages are running side by side at any given time, one reaching harvest age each day. All of them have to already be in place on day one — the experiment can’t wait r Daphnia_max_age_eaten_days days for the rotation to ramp up — so it has to start with r Daphnia_jars_needed_at_start cohort jars (r Daphnia_jars_per_cohort jars/cohort × r Daphnia_cohorts_in_rotation cohorts), each stocked with r Daphnia_adults_per_new_cohort_jar adults. That means r Daphnia_adults_needed_at_start adult Daphnia (r Daphnia_adults_per_new_cohort_jar adults/jar × r Daphnia_jars_per_cohort jars/cohort × r Daphnia_cohorts_in_rotation cohorts) are needed to start the experiment.

That stock has to be propagated up from Vana’s existing cultures. Starting from her r Vana_Daphnia_starting_jars jars, at r Daphnia_cohorts_adults_per_jar adults per jar (both placeholders — see To do below), every adult produces r Daphnia_offsprings_per_mother_per_5_days neonates per r Daphnia_max_age_eaten_days-day cycle, enough to found r Daphnia_offsprings_per_mother_per_5_days new jars per existing jar each cycle regardless of jar size (jar size cancels out of that ratio). Compounding at that rate, the r Daphnia_adults_needed_at_start-adult target is cleared within r Daphnia_propagation_cycles cycles (r Daphnia_propagation_days days) — comfortably inside the r Propagation_lead_time_days-day, one-month lead time, with r Daphnia_propagation_slack_days days to spare. Daphnia fecundity is therefore never the bottleneck: propagating every offspring into a new jar for the full r Daphnia_propagation_cycles cycles would leave r format(Daphnia_propagation_jars, big.mark = ",") jars (r format(round(Daphnia_propagation_adults), big.mark = ",") adults) — far more than needed. In practice, only enough jars should be founded in the final cycle to reach the r Daphnia_jars_needed_at_start cohort jars (r Daphnia_adults_needed_at_start adults) the rotation actually needs at the start of the experiment; the remaining lead time is buffer for losses, delays, or a smaller starting stock than assumed here.

Each of the r Daphnia_jars_needed_at_start active cohort jars is fed daily at r Daphnia_algae_C_ad_libitum_mg_per_L_per_day mg C/L into its r Large_jar_liquid_litres L of medium, which comes to r Daphnia_cohort_carbon_mg_per_day mg C/day split across the r Treatments treatment diets (Table 1 in Experimental Design). Averaged evenly across treatments, that puts daily demand at r round(Phyto_Microcystis_mg_C_per_day, 1) mg C of M. aeruginosa, r round(Phyto_Synechococcus_mg_C_per_day, 1) mg C of S. elongatus, and r round(Phyto_Nannochloropsis_mg_C_per_day, 1) mg C of N. limnetica. The stock jars that found these cohorts (r Jars_big_Daphnia large + r Jars_small_Daphnia small, fed r Daphnia_algae_C_ad_libitum_mg_per_L_per_day mg C/L/day of T. obliquus alone) add another r round(Daphnia_stock_carbon_mg_per_day, 1) mg C/day, for a combined T. obliquus demand of r round(Phyto_Tetradesmus_mg_C_per_day, 1) mg C/day.

Harvesting alternates daily across the r Phyto_vessels_per_species experimental bottles per species, so on any given day only one bottle per species is harvested while the other r Phyto_vessels_per_species - 1 regrow; whatever volume is taken out is replaced on the spot with fresh COMBO, the same way stock cultures are topped up (protocol/4_feed_Daphnia.md). At the conservative r Phyto_experimental_bottle_highest_mg_C_per_L mg C/L ceiling used throughout (r round(Phyto_experimental_bottle_max_mg_C) mg C in a full r round(Phyto_experimental_bottle_liquid_mL / 1000, 1) L bottle), that single daily bottle needs to supply r round(Phyto_Microcystis_harvest_fraction * 100)% of its standing carbon for M. aeruginosa, r round(Phyto_Synechococcus_harvest_fraction * 100)% for S. elongatus, r round(Phyto_Nannochloropsis_harvest_fraction * 100)% for N. limnetica, and r round(Phyto_Tetradesmus_harvest_fraction * 100)% for T. obliquus — all under 100%, so r Phyto_vessels_per_species bottles per species are enough to cover the carbon demand. What this doesn’t yet check is whether the diluted bottle actually regrows back to r Phyto_experimental_bottle_highest_mg_C_per_L mg C/L before its next harvest, r Phyto_vessels_per_species days later — that depends on each species’ growth rate, which isn’t pinned down yet (see To do below), and matters most for N. limnetica, whose bottle is diluted the most.

Carbon content from optical density

Daphnia is fed a fixed carbon dose (r Daphnia_algae_C_ad_libitum_mg_per_L_per_day mg C/L/day), but the volume of phytoplankton medium that delivers that dose isn’t fixed — carbon content drifts from batch to batch as a culture grows. Rather than measuring carbon directly before every feeding, which is destructive and slow, each phytoplankton species is instead read on a spectrophotometer at OD480 (see the Daphnia feeding protocol), and that reading is converted to carbon through a species-specific linear calibration curve, mg C/L = slope × OD480 − intercept, fitted by pairing OD readings across a dilution series against carbon measured on filtered, dried subsamples (method: OD curve protocol, worked through there for M. aeruginosa).

Chaoborus / Ischnura

  • Chaoborus will be sourced in the form of third-instar larvae from IT-Ichthyo Trophic, Poland.
  • Ischnura elegans will be sourced from mesocosms that had been added with freshly hatched Ischnura larvae from the field (Kastrup Fortress, Copenhagen, Denmark). The mesocosms are located in the University of Copenhagen, Denmark. They will be kept on straws.

Both Chaoborus and Ischnura will be left starving for 48-96 hours to acclimate to the new environment, let the weak individuals die, and their guts to clean before starting the experiment.

Assemble experiment

The initial population density of Chaoborus will be r Chaoborus_larvae_per_jar larvae per jar, above the r Chaoborus_one_measurement larvae per jar needed to yield enough biomass for all the different analyses (r round(Biomass_needed_total_mg, 2) mg per treatment × replicate combination, accounting for a r Chaoborus_dead_to_alive_ratio_worst_treatment-fold worst-case dead-to-alive ratio; see Box 1: Sample Collection and Storage). Scaled to r Treatments treatments × r Replicates replicates, that means r Chaoborus_total larvae in total across r Jars_Chaoborus jars. This density is below 50 individuals per jar (9 cm diameter), corresponding to ~8,000 individuals m⁻² — within the upper range of peak natural Chaoborus densities reported from eutrophic, fish-free systems [@xie1998].

Feed Chaoborus

TipProtocol 1

Chaoborus will be fed ad libitum by providing r Daphnia_neonates_needed_per_jar_per_day Daphnia of 1 to r Daphnia_max_age_eaten_days days old per jar per day (see Box 1: Sample Collection and Storage).
